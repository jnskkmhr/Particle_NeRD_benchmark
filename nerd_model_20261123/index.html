PARTICLE NERD MODEL · RETRAINED · PARTICLE SLEEPING

Closed-loop comparison with implicit MPM.

The same object, motion and sand bed are simulated twice: once with implicit MPM and once with the Particle NeRD model coupled to the same rigid-body solver. Each case has a simulation video, a plot video, a surface-distance heat-map video and a kinetic-energy figure. This page currently holds one case, the straight push of the box; the other cases will be added.

1. Protocol

Model. particle_nerd, checkpoint d4cbed5e2d4ba8af… (training step 12,000). It was trained from scratch on reference episodes simulated with implicit MPM: a rigid object on the bed (lateral push, vertical push, impact, rest) and sand only (free fall, resting bed), all recorded in one coupled scene. A share of the training steps are long windows of sand at rest. MPM is used only to produce the training data; it does not run inside the Particle NeRD solver.

Particle sleeping. The solver keeps a particle at its position, with zero velocity, when the model predicts it to move slower than 0.02 m/s (sleep_speed = 0.02). This is meant to stop the slow sinking of a bed at rest.

Difference from the 15 November page. That page used the earlier checkpoint fb443cbd06b4e9d3… without particle sleeping. The scene, the motion and the analysis are the same.

Scenes. Tasks IsaacContrib-ParticleNeRDLocal-Box-v0 (Particle NeRD) and …-Box-MPM-v0 (implicit MPM), one environment each, 200 Hz physics, seed 42. Sand: 20,646 particles on a 0.032 m lattice in a 2.0 × 1.2 × 0.30 m tray, density 1,600 kg/m³, friction 0.48. Every run starts with a 3 s hover over the bed, then performs the motion; both simulators use the same schedule. The object is a dynamic rigid body; the sand's reaction acts on it.

Commands. Run from the repository root, once per solver, then the two analyses:

uv run python scripts/neural/benchmark/run_case.py --shape box --motion push --solver nerd --checkpoint <checkpoint>
uv run python scripts/neural/benchmark/run_case.py --shape box --motion push --solver mpm
uv run python scripts/neural/benchmark/kinetic_energy.py logs/neural/benchmark/box-push
uv run --with scikit-image python scripts/neural/benchmark/surface_distance.py logs/neural/benchmark/box-push

Videos. Simulation: the Isaac Lab viewer recording of the two runs side by side. Plot: a side slab of particles coloured by displacement, and the top-view change in surface height, drawn from the saved particle states. Surface distance: the signed distance from the Particle NeRD surface to the MPM surface, defined below.

Surface distance. Particle masses are deposited on a shared 0.02 m grid and smoothed with a 0.032 m Gaussian kernel; the surface is the 800 kg/m³ iso-surface. For each upward-facing point of the NeRD surface the heat map shows the distance to the MPM iso-surface, positive where the NeRD surface lies outside the MPM material (higher or protruding) and negative where it lies inside (lower). The curves below the map are the mean and 95th-percentile absolute distance over all plotted points. The colour scale saturates at ±40 mm.

Kinetic energy. Particle speeds are finite differences of the saved positions over 20 ms, and the kinetic energy is ½ m v² with m = 0.0524 kg per particle. The figure has two panels: total kinetic energy over time (log scale), and the share of time-integrated kinetic energy at each z position (the tray floor is at −0.30 m and the original bed surface is near 0).

Bed surface before contact. The bed surface height is the highest particle in each 0.03 m column, averaged over the bed away from the tray walls, at the start of the motion (after the 3 s hover). The reported value is the Particle NeRD height minus the MPM height.

Limits: one run per case and one seed. MPM is not reproducible bit for bit once the object touches the sand, so its numbers differ slightly from the 15 November run. The sand material is a simulation setting, not calibrated to a measured specimen.

4. What the box push shows

Bed at rest. With particle sleeping the sand away from the box does not move: its surface changes by 0.0 mm during the 2.8 s motion (MPM: −1.5 mm). The slow sinking of the resting bed is gone.

Offset before contact. The sleeping bed also does not move during the 3 s hover, while the MPM bed compacts under its own weight. At the start of the motion the Particle NeRD surface is therefore 7.6 mm above the MPM surface, and the surface-distance curve starts at about 9 mm before the box touches the sand. Without sleeping the same checkpoint starts 4.7 mm above MPM.

Object. The box ends at −0.028 m with Particle NeRD and −0.026 m with MPM (15 November page: −0.032 m and −0.025 m), and at x = +0.45 m against +0.43 m.

Trench. Particle NeRD disturbs the surface more than MPM: 57.0 mm RMS height change near the object against 42.3 mm. In the plot video the trench is wider, and the berm that MPM builds in front of the box is less distinct. The sand carries 2.10 J·s of kinetic energy integrated over time against 1.25 J·s for MPM; the earlier checkpoint carried less than MPM (1.04 J·s).

Surface distance. 11.4 mm averaged over time and 13.4 mm at the end. This is larger than on the 15 November page (8.4 mm and 11.2 mm). The table below separates the two changes, the checkpoint and the sleeping; the larger distance comes with sleeping, through the offset before contact.
