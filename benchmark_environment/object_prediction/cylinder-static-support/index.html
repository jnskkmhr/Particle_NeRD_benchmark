1. Simulation conditions and evaluation method

Shared model, training conditions, and held-out test results

Table 1: Conditions for this recorded-motion comparison.
ItemDefinition
Reference motionCylinder, dynamic, drop; reference duration 20 s
Evaluated interval0–20 s; 4000/4000 steps
Particle initializationIdentical initial MPM positions; repeated initial position supplies zero-velocity history. Subsequent predictions are never replaced by MPM positions.
Body inputAt entry to the MPM step, after proxy rewind, before material solve
Body response in force reconstructionZero body mobility for prescribed objects; recorded physical mass and inertia for dynamic objects. Particle velocities use forward differences of consecutive positions.
Particle updateEvery particle is evaluated; no active-particle selection, displacement gate, particle projection, or force filter.
Regional metricsNear-contact reference distance ≤0.05 m; moving reference speed >0.01 m/s. Regions use the same reference-defined particle IDs.
Mass measurement beneath bodyHorizontal rectangle enclosing the body's world x/y projection, from 0 to 0.10 m below its lowest world z; clipped at the rigid floor z = -0.30 m
Surface reconstructionGrid 0.02 m; sensitivity grid 0.03 m; Gaussian σ=0.032 m; density threshold 800 kg/m³; 4096 area-uniform samples per direction.
Force reconstructionNormal and tangential material effective-mass factors 10 and 10; stabilization 0.01; collider friction 1.

The reference body moves dynamically or follows its prescribed trajectory during MPM recording. During evaluation, its recorded poses and solver-entry free velocities are imposed on Particle NeRD. The recorded free velocities retain information from the MPM coupling. Estimated forces do not change body motion; these results do not establish closed-loop stability.
