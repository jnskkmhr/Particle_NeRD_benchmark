1. Physical definitions: compressive pressure $p_i$ and deviatoric shear stress $\boldsymbol{\tau}_i$

In continuum granular mechanics, the $3 \times 3$ Cauchy stress tensor $\boldsymbol{\sigma}_i$ at particle $i$ decomposes into two physically distinct parts:

$$\boldsymbol{\sigma}_i = -p_i\,\mathbf{I} + \boldsymbol{\tau}_i, \qquad p_i = -\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_i) \ge 0, \qquad \operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_i) = 0$$
  • Compressive pressure $p_i \ge 0$ ($\text{Pa}$ or $\text{kPa}$): The isotropic normal squeeze acting inward from all directions on particle $i$. Because dry cohesionless sand cannot sustain tension ($p_i \ge 0$), $p_i$ is governed by three physical effects:
    1. Hydrostatic overburden $p_{\text{hydro}}(z_i) = \rho g (-z_i)$, which increases linearly with depth below the free surface;
    2. Sub-collider Boussinesq bearing bulb, where an external load on the sand surface (such as an impacting box or stepping robot foot) transmits a bulb-shaped high-pressure zone into the supporting particle column directly beneath the collider; and
    3. Dynamic compaction ($\dot{\phi}_i = d\phi_i / dt > 0$), where converging particles compress the local pore volume.
  • Deviatoric shear stress $\boldsymbol{\tau}_i$ and its magnitude $\|\boldsymbol{\tau}_i\|$ ($\text{Pa}$): The trace-free (anisotropic) part of the stress tensor that causes shape distortion and plastic shear sliding along internal slip planes without changing volume. In granular media (Drucker–Prager / Mohr–Coulomb plasticity), shear failure occurs when the deviatoric stress reaches the friction yield cone $\|\boldsymbol{\tau}_i\| \le \mu_{\text{internal}}\,p_i$, localizing along the shear band beneath and around the edges of an intruding body.

2. Dynamic free-projectile impact & support (projectile-flat): Implicit MPM vs. Particle-NeRD

Why evaluate forces and stresses on a dynamic simulation? In a prescribed (kinematic) motion, the rigid body is forced along a fixed trajectory with infinite effective stiffness regardless of material resistance, which can create artificial impulse spikes from tiny position discrepancies. For a fair physical comparison against Implicit MPM, this evaluation uses the fully dynamic $2.0\text{ kg}$ free-projectile simulation (projectile-flat):

  • Dynamic motion protocol & object trajectory: A $2.0\text{ kg}$ rigid box ($0.26 \times 0.18 \times 0.05\text{ m}$, kinematic_enabled = False) is held at $(-0.55, 0.0, 0.35)\text{ m}$ until $t = 1.0\text{ s}$ and then released as a free projectile with initial linear velocity $\mathbf{v}_0 = (1.0, 0.0, 0.4)\text{ m/s}$ and angular velocity $\boldsymbol{\omega}_0 = (0.0, 1.0, 0.0)\text{ rad/s}$. 3. Unified Packing-State + 3D RFT contact scaling

Instead of using four separate hard-coded box mass scales, the contact estimator determines each contacting particle's effective mobilized inertia $m_{\text{eff}, i}$ from (1) its local Wendland packing sum $\phi_i$ and compression rate $\dot{\epsilon}_{v,i}$, and (2) a continuous 3D Resistive Force Theory (RFT) directional multiplier $\alpha_{\text{RFT}}(\beta_i, \gamma_i)$ over surface orientation $\beta_i$ and velocity dive angle $\gamma_i$:

$$s_{\text{pack}, i} - 1 = s_0 \left(\frac{\phi_i}{\phi_{\text{ref}}}\right)^2 \bigl(1 + 4\,\dot{\epsilon}_{v,i}\bigr), \qquad m_{\text{eff}, n, i} = m_i \Bigl(1 + (s_{\text{pack}, i} - 1)\,\alpha_{\text{RFT}, n}(\beta_i, \gamma_i)\Bigr)$$
