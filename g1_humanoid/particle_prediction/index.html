1. Particle prediction under prescribed robot motion

At every 0.005 s step, prescribe the recorded robot motion and predict particle positions recurrently with the current NeRD solver. Compare each predicted particle with the same particle ID in the MPM recording. The 3D position RMSE is √mean(‖predicted position − MPM position‖²) over 88,360 particles. Mean signed z error is mean(predicted z − MPM z); positive means higher than the corresponding MPM particle.

Argon’s foot-speed and particle-height displacement rule is enabled. Active selection, background motion and boundary handling are included. This measures the complete particle-update method, rather than the raw network output. A bed-wide mean can hide local errors beneath the feet.

2. Full closed-loop particle simulation

Here the same policy controls two independent robots: one coupled to native implicit MPM and the other to Particle NeRD. Both start from identical particle and robot states. The plots compare deformation relative to each run’s own initial particles. Because the robot trajectories can diverge, this is a full-simulation comparison, not isolated particle-prediction accuracy.

Full-simulation conditions, stated separately from the prescribed-motion tests.
ControllerMPM-trained G1 policy jqur5jhm, checkpoint 19,999; deterministic inference. Same policy and seed 42. Five commands have paired MPM references; the negative-yaw case retains its NeRD-only recording.
DynamicsFor each of the five paired commands, two independent rollouts: MJWarp + implicit MPM, and MJWarp + current Particle NeRD. Identical rigid solver, foot proxy mapping and lagged coupling. Each policy receives its own simulated observations; its actions and robot trajectory may diverge. No prescribed robot or particle trajectory.
InitializationBit-identical initial particle positions and rigid-body poses, checked from saved arrays. Robot base starts at (0, 0, 0.76) m. Both start from the fresh grid without settling warm-up. NeRD runs zero native MPM steps and loads no MPM trajectory; a runtime guard rejects native-MPM solver construction. The separate MPM run evolves the sand with native implicit MPM.
Sand bed / resolution6 m × 6 m × 0.3 m, widened from the task’s 6 m × 2 m bed to allow lateral motion; 2,671,875 particles. Both solvers use voxel size 0.02 m and identical material parameters: density 1,600 kg/m³, friction 0.48, Young’s modulus 10¹⁵ Pa, Poisson ratio 0.3 and yield pressure 10¹⁵ Pa. All material fields are checked against the metadata.
Particle selection / stabilizationNeRD: 7,104 active particles and 512 background samples, with the current foot-speed and particle-height displacement rule enabled. MPM: every particle is evolved by the native solver; no neural network or displacement gate. Neither run adds a force filter.
Timing200 Hz physics, 50 Hz control, 50 fps video. Six NeRD episodes and five MPM references (negative-yaw reference omitted), requested duration 6 s per episode; stop on termination or when |base x| or |base y| exceeds 2.6 m. Plots retain each run’s full recorded duration. Paired videos stop at the shorter recording; individual complete videos remain linked. Failures are retained.
Implicit MPM settingsSparse grid, zero padding; APIC transfer; P0 strain and Q1 velocity basis; pic27 collider basis; forward collider velocity; at most 25 iterations, tolerance 10⁻⁴; automatic warm start. Rigid solver uses two substeps per 0.005 s exchange. Full solver settings and source hashes are linked for each run.

Data and reproduction

Raw arrays and particle-state datasets remain in the local archive. The local artifact manifest lists their paths, sizes and checksums; these files are not public downloads.

All results use the installed checkpoint and solver source recorded in their metadata. No training was performed for this report. Prescribed-motion accuracy is measured on one archived MPM trajectory; full simulation is measured on one episode per solver and command. The different bed dimensions, resolutions and initial states are documented above and are not pooled into a single accuracy number.
