1. Simulation conditions

This is a new evaluation of the currently installed Particle NeRD solver and checkpoint. The reference is an existing implicit-MPM recording of G1 walking on a fixed sand bed. Particle NeRD predicts the sand motion while following that same recorded robot motion.

2. Evaluation method

At each 0.005 s step, the solver receives the recorded collider poses and velocities and its own predicted particle history. The force estimator also receives the recorded proxy free velocity, reconstructed by removing the previous material-force impulse from the advanced body velocity. Particle predictions are carried forward for the entire five seconds, without replacing them with MPM particles.

The robot follows prescribed motion. It does not respond to the newly estimated NeRD forces. Both video panels therefore show identical recorded G1 poses; their particle states differ. The video uses saved states from this evaluation, and its time label uses the same elapsed time as the plots. Elapsed 0 s corresponds to recording time 0.300 s.

Match each predicted particle to the same particle ID in the MPM recording. At every timestep, compute the square root of the mean squared 3D position distance over 88,360 particles. The signed z error is the mean of predicted z minus MPM z over those same particles: negative means particles are lower than their MPM counterparts.

These are errors of the complete current solver, including active selection, background motion, displacement scaling, and boundary handling. They are not errors of the raw neural-network output alone. A bed-wide average can hide larger local errors near the feet.
