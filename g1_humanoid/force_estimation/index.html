1. Simulation conditions

This is a new evaluation of the currently installed Particle NeRD solver and checkpoint. The reference is an existing implicit-MPM recording of G1 walking on a fixed sand bed. Particle NeRD predicts the sand motion while following that same recorded robot motion.

2. Evaluation method

At each 0.005 s step, the solver receives the recorded collider poses and velocities and its own predicted particle history. The force estimator also receives the recorded proxy free velocity, reconstructed by removing the previous material-force impulse from the advanced body velocity. Particle predictions are carried forward for the entire five seconds, without replacing them with MPM particles.

The robot follows prescribed motion. It does not respond to the newly estimated NeRD forces. Both video panels therefore show identical recorded G1 poses; their particle states differ. The video uses saved states from this evaluation, and its time label uses the same elapsed time as the plots. Elapsed 0 s corresponds to recording time 0.300 s.

For each foot and timestep, compare the predicted sand-on-foot force with the recorded MPM sand-on-foot force. Forces are expressed in the world frame, in newtons; positive z is upward. The force-vector RMSE is the square root of the mean of (ΔFx² + ΔFy² + ΔFz²), averaged over two feet and 1,000 timesteps. Every force sample is plotted without temporal smoothing.

Why the left foot loses support in this replay

During elapsed 0.8–2.0 s, mean NeRD Fz is 0.45 N on the left foot and 149.86 N on the right; MPM gives 166.54 N and 160.88 N. This early loss of left-foot support is a prediction failure, rather than ordinary alternating walking contact.

At 1.50 s, the nearest predicted particle center directly below the left sole is 26.5 mm from the sole, measured along the foot’s local normal. The corresponding MPM distance is 1.8 mm. The right-foot distances are 10.1 mm for NeRD and 5.0 mm for MPM. The left-foot particle layer has moved too far from the sole to provide the required support.

A controlled force-estimator check at 1.50 s keeps the recorded body motion and estimator parameters fixed. Supplying MPM particle positions and velocities produces Fz = 153.9 N left / 168.2 N right, close to the MPM reference of 155.7 / 170.7 N. Supplying the NeRD particle motion produces 0 / 165.1 N. The diagnostic reproduces the first 600 plotted steps exactly.

Argon’s displacement rule is enabled. At 0.80 s its foot-speed multiplier is 1.00 for the left foot and 0.31 for the right, so the slowly settling feet are treated differently. By 1.50 s the left-foot multiplier is zero, but stopping further particle motion does not refill the gap. This identifies a limitation of this replay; it does not by itself isolate the network, active selection, and displacement rule as separate causes.

The recorded robot trajectory continues despite the missing NeRD support force because this is a prescribed-motion evaluation. A robot driven by these estimated forces would be free to deviate from that trajectory. Diagnostic measurements and conditions.
