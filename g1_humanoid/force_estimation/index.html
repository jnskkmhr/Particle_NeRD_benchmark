1. Force estimator with prescribed particles

Supply recorded robot motion and recorded MPM particle positions at every step. Particle velocities are computed from consecutive recorded positions divided by 0.005 s, matching the current reconstruction’s position-to-velocity convention. Apply the current active-particle selector and force estimator. No neural particle prediction or displacement stabilization is applied. The estimated forces do not alter the recorded robot motion. As specified above, the pre-contact proxy velocity is reconstructed using the prior recorded MPM wrench; this is a conditional reconstruction test.

For each timestep and foot, subtract the recorded MPM force vector. Force-vector RMSE = √mean(ΔFx² + ΔFy² + ΔFz²), averaged over both feet and all 1,000 timesteps. This test also retains the current contact selection and finite-difference velocity convention.

2. Force estimator with NeRD-predicted particles

Keep the same recorded robot motion, but let the current NeRD solver predict particle positions recurrently for five seconds. MPM particle positions initialize only the first three history frames. Subsequent NeRD particle states are carried forward without replacement by MPM. The force estimator reads these predicted particles. NeRD forces do not move the robot in this test.

The current foot-speed and particle-height displacement rule is enabled. These results evaluate the integrated predictor, active selection, displacement scaling, boundary handling and force reconstruction together.

Force-vector RMSE: 83.60 N. The same estimator gives 32.66 N with prescribed particles. Their difference is not an additive decomposition of error; particle geometry changes contact admission and force response nonlinearly.

Why left and right support differ in this replay

At elapsed 1.50 s, MPM gives Fz = 155.7 N left / 170.7 N right. NeRD-predicted particles give 0 / 165.1 N; feeding MPM particle motion to the same estimator gives 153.9 / 168.2 N. The nearest particle center directly below the left sole is 26.5 mm from the sole in NeRD, versus 1.8 mm in MPM, measured along the foot’s local normal. These are particle-center distances, not surface clearances.

The displacement rule can stop further motion beneath a slow foot, but it does not refill an existing gap. The early left-support loss is a failure in this prescribed-motion test. It does not identify the network, particle selection and stabilization rule as separate causes. Detailed diagnostic measurements.

3. Full closed-loop simulation

Run the policy and coupled rigid–particle dynamics freely. Estimated sand forces affect the robot, and the resulting robot motion affects the next particle prediction. The six commands below are separate fresh episodes, not segments joined into one trajectory.

Full-simulation conditions, stated separately from the prescribed-motion tests.

Data and reproduction

Raw arrays and particle-state datasets remain in the local archive. The local artifact manifest lists their paths, sizes and checksums; these files are not public downloads.

All results use the installed checkpoint and solver source recorded in their metadata. No training was performed for this report. Prescribed-motion accuracy is measured on one archived MPM trajectory; full simulation is measured on one episode per command. The different bed dimensions, resolutions and initial states are documented above and are not pooled into a single accuracy number.
